# Physical features that shaped history and still matter: an independent selection

Prepared 2026-10-12. I picked these from first principles. I did not consult any existing deck, list or file in this directory.

**How to read the source keys.** Every key is defined in the [Source list](#source-list). Each source carries one of these tags:
- **[V]**: I opened the page and checked the figure at its primary source.
- **[S]**: I confirmed the claim from search-indexed text of the named source but did not open the full page. britannica.com returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches, so every Britannica claim is [S].
- **[M]**: a book cited from memory. I did not open it during this session.
- **GK**: general historical knowledge that I did not check against a source this session. Treat these rows as needing a check before they go on a card (see [Verify flags](#verify-flags)).

## 1. Criteria

Each candidate is scored 0–3 on five axes. The letters after each tier show which axes scored 3, or 2 or more for **N**.

| Code | Criterion | A "3" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | **Population and civilization.** A large share of humanity lives on it, or a primary civilization formed around it. | Nile, Ganges, North China Plain |
| **T** | **Trade and energy flow.** It carries or carried a major share of world trade, oil, gas or grain, or a defining historic trade route. | Hormuz, Malacca, Silk Road corridors |
| **M** | **Military.** It is a repeated invasion route, a defensive barrier, or the site of decisive battles across several eras. | North European Plain, Khyber, Alps |
| **B** | **Borders and cultural spread.** It fixes a lasting political or civilizational boundary, or acts as a conduit for religions, languages and empires. | Pyrenees, Himalayas, Sahara |
| **N** | **Now.** It is strategically live in the 2020s: an active dispute, a chokepoint under stress, or a water or energy security issue. | Taiwan Strait, Black Sea, Suwałki Gap |

**Tier rules, applied consistently:**
- **Tier 1:** scores 3 on at least two axes, with N ≥ 2. One exception: a civilizational cradle (P = 3 and B = 3) is Tier 1 even with a lower N, because the deck is also about history.
- **Tier 2:** scores 3 on one axis, or 2 on three or more.
- **Tier 3:** scores 2 on one or two axes. Real, but narrower in time, place or consequence.

**Counts:** 169 features (Tier 1: 54, Tier 2: 94, Tier 3: 21). That includes 16 climate systems (5 / 7 / 4). Climate systems use the same tier rules, with P/T/M/B/N reinterpreted as described in §11. The total is above the 100–150 target. If you need to trim, the Tier 3 rows go first.

**Other rules:**
- Prefer the feature over the human artifact. Canals are included because the prompt asks for them. Human routes such as the Silk Road are carded through their physical corridors (Hexi Corridor, Dzungarian Gate), not as features in their own right.
- When two features always work as a pair, merge them into one row (Tigris–Euphrates, Magellan/Cape Horn) to avoid duplicate cards.
- **Card rule:** every feature must render as one map with one highlighted place, answered by its name. Anything that needs two maps (seasonal or anomaly pairs) or has no single locatable extent is out, or is redefined as its main region.

## 11. Climate systems (winds, currents, oscillations, storm belts)

**Scope.** This category covers recurring atmospheric and oceanic systems that are tied to particular places and shaped settlement, trade, war or agriculture. One-off weather events are out.

**How the five criteria are adapted.**
- **P (Population and civilization)** becomes: the system controls the food supply or habitability of a large population (monsoon rains, upwelling fisheries, famine teleconnections).
- **T (Trade)** becomes: it set the sailing routes of the Age of Sail, or it drives fisheries and shipping risk today.
- **M (Military)** becomes: it decided campaigns or fleets.
- **B (Borders and spread)** becomes: it carried peoples, religions and empires along wind-set routes, or it draws a lasting climatic boundary (the monsoon Asia boundary, desert belts).
- **N (Now)** becomes: it is a live driver of food, water, disaster or climate-change risk in the 2020s.

The tier rules are unchanged.

27. **"About 70% of India's rain" and "about half of farmland rain-fed".** These are rounded figures from news coverage of IMD data, not an IMD publication.
