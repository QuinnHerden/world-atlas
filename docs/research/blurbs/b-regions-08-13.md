# Blurbs: regions 8–13 (Arabia to Southern Africa)

> **Research for World Atlas P2.7b, 2026-10-14.** Blurbs (the `note` field) for every country and capital card in ring regions 8–13 of [world-atlas-plan.md](../../world-atlas-plan.md): Arabian Peninsula, Horn & East Africa, North Africa, West Africa, Central Africa, and Southern Africa & Indian Ocean. Regions run in ring order and countries in walk order. Each capital follows its country. Dispute pockets (Somaliland, Western Sahara) are written by another stream and are not here.

## Conventions

- **Slug** is the country slug, shared by the country and capital rows, so the id tags are `id_country_<slug>` and `id_capital_<slug>`.
- **Keys** name the sources for the blurb's claims (see [Sources](#sources)):
  - **[V]** means verified at a source that was actually opened.
  - **[S]** means the claim was seen only in search-result text.
  - **FLAG** marks a row listed under [Flags](#flags).
- **Geography claims** such as "on the coast", "on the X River", "near the Y border" or "in the centre" are measured from the vendored Natural Earth layers (key **NE-GEO**; method under Sources), not taken from prose.
- **Figures.** The blurbs contain no measurements or statistics. Years appear only where a key verifies them.
- **Time-sensitive** wording is marked "(as of 2026-10, verify)" inside the blurb. Strip the marker after P3 review if the fact still holds.

## Flags

Decisions for the user come first, then the time-sensitive items.

**Capital choice (user decision; these differ from the plan's capital list or NE's Admin-0 tag):**

1. **Burundi: Gitega, not Bujumbura.** Factbook: "Gitega (political capital), Bujumbura (commercial capital)"; parliament voted for Gitega in January 2019, and the move was "incomplete" as of 2023. NE tags Bujumbura (`1159150427`) as `Admin-0 capital`. Gitega exists only as `Admin-1 capital` `1159146001`. Under the official-capital rule the card should be Gitega. The plan's multi-capital list leaves Burundi out, so add it.
2. **Equatorial Guinea: Ciudad de la Paz, not Malabo.** By a decree in early January 2026, Ciudad de la Paz became the capital. Camer.be says it was signed on 2 January; AAP says "issued on Saturday", which was 3 January. The factbook mirror predates the decree and still says Malabo. NE has **no populated_places row** for Ciudad de la Paz or Oyala, so the card needs a hand-placed point, as Yaren does. Two items were seen only in search or Wikipedia leads ([S]), so the blurb uses neither:
   - a September 2026 AFP report calling the city a near-empty "ghost town";
   - the decree's title on guineaecuatorialpress.com, whose page came back empty when fetched. If the user keeps Malabo, use this blurb: *"Malabo, on the island of Bioko in the Bight of Biafra, was Equatorial Guinea's capital until January 2026, when a decree named Ciudad de la Paz on the mainland as capital. The move of government is still under way (as of 2026-10, verify)."* [V] FB-EK, AAP-2026.
3. **Eswatini: Mbabane kept.** Factbook: "Mbabane (administrative capital); Lobamba (royal and legislative capital)". NE tags Mbabane as `Admin-0 capital` and Lobamba as `capital alt` (`1159146343`). The 2005 Constitution names no capital city:
   - s.229(3) makes "the official residence of the Ndlovukazi" the "legislative and ceremonial capital";
   - s.133(2) leaves Parliament's seat to a royal Gazette notice.

   Mbabane's status therefore rests on the factbook and NE. The blurb keeps Mbabane and names Lobamba. Confirm.
4. **Tanzania: Dodoma.** This follows the plan. The factbook calls Dar es Salaam "administrative capital" only in its Major urban areas field. The Citizen (13 Oct 2019) reports that all ministries, the prime minister and the president had moved to Dodoma by 2019, so the blurb follows that. **NE tags Dodoma only `Admin-0 capital alt` (`1159149731`)**, so the card must use the alt row, as in Burundi. The newest source is from 2019; no 2026 government source was reached.

**Time-sensitive (as of 2026-10, verify):**

5. **Sudan:** the war that began in 2023 (FB-SU, Military section). The government announced its return from Port Sudan to Khartoum, effective 11 January 2026 (Dabanga via AllAfrica, 12 Jan 2026; the date is inferred from "Sunday" in an article dated the 12th). Sources disagree on how complete the return is.
6. **Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger:** their withdrawal from ECOWAS took effect on 29 January 2025 (ECOWAS press statement). ECOWAS named the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) of the three on 29 March 2026. The press statement is dated 29 January; the page shows it posted on 30 January. Their military governments are from FB-ML, FB-UV and FB-NG.
7. **Yemen:** Houthi control of Sanaa since 2014, and the war (FB-YM). Wikipedia leads only, not evidence: the war continues, and fighting resumed in September–October 2026. The recognised government's seat (Aden) is unverified, so the blurb leaves it out.
8. **Libya:** rival governments in the east and west (FB-LY). A Wikipedia lead, from May 2026, says both still stand.
9. **DR Congo:** the M23 and the eastern conflict (FB-CG). No 2026 source was found, so the blurb uses "have long fought", which won't go stale.
10. **Mauritius:** the Chagos claim. The UK–Mauritius treaty was signed in May 2025 and has not been ratified; the plan records it as shelved as of May 2026. "Claims" stays true either way.

**Deliberately left out:**

- Egypt's New Administrative Capital. The factbook doesn't mention it, and I couldn't check it once the search budget ran out. Cairo stays the capital.
- Recent coups not used as hooks: Guinea-Bissau (November 2025, FB-PU) and Madagascar (October 2025, FB-MA). Their blurbs avoid them so they won't go stale.
- The "1781" founding year the factbook gives for Freetown is suspect (the usual date is 1787), so the blurb has no year.
- Las Anod and Somaliland's recognition belong on the Somaliland dispute card (plan decision 10), not on Somalia's.

**Observation outside this scope:** in NE populated_places, Laayoune (in Western Sahara) is tagged `Admin-0 capital alt` with ADM0NAME Morocco. The Western Sahara and Morocco stream should check that tag.

**Fact-check.** A second researcher checked a 26% sample (32 of 122 rows), every FLAG row and flags 1–10 against the keyed sources. It found 9 fixes, all applied:

- **Errors:**
  - UAE: six emirates formed it in 1971; Ras al-Khaimah joined in 1972.
  - Dodoma: the blurb was out of date.
- **Claims beyond the source:** for Ciudad de la Paz, "formerly Oyala", the exact decree date and "far from complete".
- **Wording:** Somalia ("since then"), Rwanda ("against the Tutsi"), DR Congo (made durable), Mauritius ("island nation") and Mali (the Niger's course).
