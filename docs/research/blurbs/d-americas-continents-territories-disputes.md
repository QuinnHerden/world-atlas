# Grounding blurbs D: the Americas, continents, territories and disputes

World Atlas P2.7d, 2026-10-14. One blurb per card for regions 19–21 (every country, and every capital that gets a card), the 7 continents, the 23 dependent territories and the 15 disputed areas, as decided in [world-atlas-plan.md](../../world-atlas-plan.md). Each blurb is 1–3 plain sentences and goes into the card's `note`. Sections follow the ring: regions 19, 20 and 21 in walk order, then continents, territories and disputes.

**Columns.** `slug` is the card's id: the country id is shared by its country and capital rows, and `kind` tells them apart (country, capital, continent, territory, dispute). Territories and disputes get no capital rows.

**Source keys**, as in [research-independent.md](../research-independent.md):
- **[V]**: I opened the page or file and read the claim there.
- **[S]**: I confirmed the claim from search-indexed text only (britannica.com returns 403 to direct fetches, so every Britannica key is [S]).

Factbook keys (`FB-xx`) cite the community mirror `factbook/factbook.json` at commit `144d6977` (last data update 2026-01-22, before the CIA retired the Factbook). They are [V] for what the mirror says; the mirror is a scraped copy, not the CIA's own release. Wikipedia was used for leads only and is never a key. **No figures** appear unless verified; dates are used, areas and populations are not.

**Disputes** state who claims the area, who controls it and since when, and every dispute row (and every time-sensitive territory row) is marked "as of 2026-10 (verify)".

## Flags

### Regions 19 and 21

- **Belize (ICJ):** the ICJ case page lists no hearings or judgment; its latest listed step is the 24 June 2022 order. Verify the case status before release. The blurb says only that the claim "has been referred" to the Court.
- **Mexico:** the Factbook mirror uses "Gulf of America" (the 2025 US renaming). The blurb uses "Gulf of Mexico", the name Mexico and international bodies use. The user should decide on deck-wide naming.
- **Guatemala City, "inland in the southern highlands":** Britannica gives the 1776 move and the site 45 km east of Antigua [S]. The highland setting comes from Factbook terrain plus general knowledge; I did not see it stated for the city itself.
- **Saint Lucia, "between Martinique and Saint Vincent":** a standard position, but I found no indexed source saying it. Confirm it, or drop the phrase.
- **Port-au-Prince, "capital since 1770":** single [S] search snippet; confirm on Britannica's Port-au-Prince page.
- **Kingston:** Britannica's Spanish Town entry says Spanish Town was the capital from 1692 to 1872. The blurb states only that Kingston became the political capital in 1872, which both entries agree on.
- **Managua:** Britannica attributes the spread-out layout to both the 1931 and the 1972 earthquakes. Its sources differ on the 1972 death toll, so no figure is used.
- **Canada, "second-largest country":** a ranking, not a figure. It comes from Factbook's geography note [V].
- **Saint Kitts and Nevis, "smallest country in the Western Hemisphere by area and population":** Factbook [V]. It is a superlative, so recheck it if a stricter no-figures reading applies.
- **Barbados:** the Factbook mirror's government type still says "a Commonwealth realm", which is stale. The blurb follows the Factbook background and Britannica: a republic since 2021.
- **Search budget:** the session's WebSearch budget ran out at the end of this work. The Panama Canal handover wording rests on Factbook alone ("1977 agreement ... complete transfer ... by the end of the century") and does not state the 31 Dec 1999 date.
